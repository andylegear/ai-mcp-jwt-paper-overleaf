\begin{abstract}
Large Language Models operating as tool-chain orchestrators must relay
high-entropy credentials such as JSON Web Tokens (JWTs) verbatim, yet their
subword tokenisation is associated with systematic character-level corruption
across a substantial fraction of attempts. We report a controlled experiment
($N\!=\!48{,}000$ L2 runs across 16 frontier LLMs) measuring the JWT
Defect/Failure Rate (DFR), an ecological validity arm ($N\!=\!1{,}600$)
replicating the study under production conditions, and an industrial case study
validating a novel reference architecture. The overall DFR is 18.0\% (range
0.5--99.8\%; 12.5\% excluding the Mistral Large~3 outlier). Corruption is
length-gated and payload-concentrated. Even the most explicit production
instruction leaves a residual DFR of 19.9\% ($+1.9$~pp vs.\ neutral,
$p\!=\!0.16$): a null result for the prompt-engineering mitigation tested under
production conditions; formal equivalence testing remains future work. We propose the Credential Interception Vault, a
reference architecture grounded in three design principles (deterministic
shell/probabilistic core, credentials by reference, model-agnosticism) that
removes the credential from the LLM inference path entirely, achieving zero
byte-relay failures by construction and validated in a production fintech deployment.
\end{abstract}
